About Albert Camus

Albert Camus was a French-Algerian writer and philosopher. He was born in 1913 and became known for his ideas about life, freedom, and the human condition.

Camus wrote novels, essays, and plays. His writing often explored the meaning of life and the idea of the absurd. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1957.

Famous Works

  • The Stranger
  • The Plague
  • The Myth of Sisyphus

Why He Is Interesting

Camus is interesting because his writings discuss important questions about life and human choices. His ideas continue to be discussed today.
